The Counter-Intelligence Program, more commonly known as COINTELPRO, was a series of covert Federal Bureau of Investigation operations from 1956 to 1971 that targeted any domestic political groups deemed subversive, including civil rights organizations, socialist movements, and anti-war activists. The program was notable for violating the First Amendment rights of Americans through illegal wiretapping and harassment, even resulting in a number of deaths.[^11] For more information, see [Spying on America: The FBI's Domestic Counter-Intelligence Program](https://academic.oup.com/jah/article-abstract/80/1/347/672209).

[^11]: United States Senate, “U.S. Senate: Senate Select Committee to Study Governmental Operations with Respect to Intelligence Activities,” www.senate.gov, April 29, 1976, [https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/investigations/church-committee.htm](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/investigations/church-committee.htm).
