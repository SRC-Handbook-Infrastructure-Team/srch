Sustainability’s definition has evolved over the past few decades to better reflect what it means in society today. In the 1980s it focused on conservation of animals and ecosystems, whereas today it has transitioned to reflect how to address current needs while planning for the impact for generations ahead, like continued demand for natural resources, stable weather patterns, or clean air to breathe. Definitions are also transformed by their authors. The most commonly cited definition by the UN World Commission on Environment and Development focuses on what sustainability means globally and for all people.[^2] In contrast, Brown University’s Sustainability Strategic Plan emphasizes the institution's local responsibilities to ensure that our descendants' futures are not hurt by our current actions.[^3] It calls for action and policy centered on careful consideration of future generations.

### Various Definitions of Sustainability

- **Oxford Dictionary’s definition:** The property of being environmentally sustainable; the degree to which a process or enterprise is able to be maintained or continued while avoiding the long-term depletion of natural resources.[^1]
- **UN World Commission on Environment and Development’s definition:** Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.[^2]
- **Brown University’s Sustainability Strategic Plan’s definition:** Sustainability at Brown means ensuring the operations of the campus do not impair the ability of future generations (at Brown, in our community and around the world) to rely on the environmental benefits that are available today. Further, it requires that our research, teaching and community engagement increasingly incorporate sustainability solutions as an essential component of fulfilling the University’s mission.[^3]

### How do we define sustainability in the context of technology?

Technological advances have both the power and potential to enable a full green transition, and, on the other hand, hinder it through excessive waste, material, and energy consumption. As students and scholars, it's imperative to consider all the impacts technology has on our environment, both positive and negative. Throughout this section we will consider technology’s role in sustainability, and what responsible sustainability-conscious design and deployment of technology looks like.

### Environmental and Human Impacts

When referencing sustainability it is critical to acknowledge it is beyond just the environment. It involves social, economic, and environmental factors and all are of importance to maintaining a sustainable system.[^4] Our environment is the foundation of our interconnected system. Our economy relies on natural resources, materials, consistent supply chains, and agriculture to promote and sustain long term economic growth. Our society depends on access to clean air, water, education, good health, and safe shelter to build healthy and happy communities. The choices and actions we make today directly influence this system’s future success. As discussed across the primers these choices go beyond the environmental footprint, but have substantial human impacts.
